# Block P3 (Cafe) — Fictional Venue Seed Answers

Interview notes, The Batch House, Seddon. Visit conducted 2026-09-07, on site, approx. 90 minutes across a quiet mid-morning lull and a follow-up chat during the lunch service wind-down. Present: Renata Alves (owner), Jack Doran (barista, senior), Mia Fenwick (barista/FOH, part time). Priya Chandran (cook, built the menu) was not on site, rostered off Sundays.

---

## Venue profile

- **Name:** The Batch House
- **Suburb:** Seddon, VIC (inner west, Melbourne)
- **Seating:** 42 seats indoor, 8 seats on the footpath out front (weather permitting) — see note below, this number gets muddier later in the interview
- **Trading hours:** Tuesday to Sunday, 7am to 3pm. Closed Mondays. No evening trade.
- **Licensing status:** Unlicensed. No liquor licence, no BYO arrangement of any kind.
- **Food offering:** Specialty coffee (house blend from a Brunswick roaster) plus a genuine kitchen: all day brunch menu, eggs done multiple ways, toasties, a rotating lunch specials board, house made banana bread and granola, salads.
- **Staff roster:**
  - Renata Alves, owner/manager, on the floor most shifts
  - Priya Chandran, cook, built and owns the menu, also holds the venue's Food Safety Supervisor certificate (status below)
  - Jack Doran, senior barista, full time
  - Mia Fenwick, barista/FOH, part time, mostly weekends
  - Liam Suárez, kitchen hand/FOH casual
  - Sofia Nguyen, kitchen hand/FOH casual, newest hire, started three weeks ago

## Venue basics

Renata gave the legal name as "Batch House Pty Ltd," trading as The Batch House. Address is on Charles Street, Seddon, VIC 3011. She rattled off the ABN without checking anything, clearly knows it cold. Trading hours she was equally confident on: "seven to three, Tuesday to Sunday, every week, hasn't changed since we opened." Closed Mondays, no plans to change that ("Monday's our one day where nobody has to be here, I'm not touching it").

Seating: "forty two inside, eight out front on the footpath when the weather's decent." She said this early and firmly. Later, when we got to the OHS section and talked about max occupancy for a fire/evacuation scenario, Renata paused and said "actually, realistically, with the pram traffic on weekends and the way the tables get pushed around, I reckon it's closer to thirty five in here most of the time." Nobody in the room flagged that this didn't match the forty two from earlier — Jack just nodded along. Leaving both numbers in as given.

## Licensing status (root gate)

Asked directly and early, per the new flow gate: does the venue hold, or plan to hold, any liquor licence at all, including an informal BYO setup.

Renata: "No, none of that. We've never had a licence, we don't do BYO, we don't want the hassle of it. We're a coffee and brunch place, people come in early, we're done by three, there's no appetite here for wine with lunch." Confirmed this is a deliberate, settled position, not a "haven't gotten around to it yet" situation — she said they looked into a limited licence when they opened three years ago and decided against it because of the extra insurance and reporting.

No RSA holders on staff. No crowd control. No happy hour or discounted pricing of any kind — "our discount is a free coffee if you're a regular, that's about as loose as it gets." None of the RSA, crowd control, or happy hour questions were pursued further given the above, per the gate.

## Staffing

Roles confirmed: Renata (owner, does open and close most days, also covers register and floor), Priya (cook, in five days a week, off Sundays and Mondays), Jack (barista, full time, does most of the espresso machine maintenance), Mia (barista/FOH, weekends mostly plus a couple of weekday mornings), Liam and Sofia (kitchen hand/FOH casuals, rotate through toastie station and dishes).

Rostering: done by Renata in a shared Google Sheet, published about a week ahead. Swaps are handled staff to staff via a group chat, "as long as someone tells me before it happens, I don't need to approve every swap." When pressed on whether there's a formal minimum-notice policy for swaps she said "no, not written down, it's just been fine so far because it's a small team."

## Food safety / Food Safety Supervisor

Priya holds the venue's Food Safety Supervisor certificate. Renata was less sure of the details here: "I know she's got the FSS cert, I want to say she did that course maybe two years ago? I'd have to check the folder for the actual expiry date, I don't have it memorised." Flagged as something to chase before go live rather than guessed at.

Temperature logging: done for the walk in fridge and the display cabinet, but Renata admitted the cabinet side is patchy. "Mia's pretty good about logging the cabinet in the mornings when she opens. On weekends when it's just casuals running the floor it doesn't always happen, if I'm honest." No written escalation process for what happens if a reading is out of range — "we'd just chuck it and figure out why afterwards."

Cleaning schedule: exists but nobody in the room could describe it precisely. "Priya runs that, she's got a whiteboard list in the kitchen for who does what at close. You'd want to ask her for the actual breakdown, I just know it happens." Receiving checks on deliveries: informal, whoever's on kitchen that morning checks temperature on the fridge/dairy delivery "by feel mostly, we don't log it against a number every time."

Given the flow's weighting note that food safety carries most of the real compliance risk here with no alcohol licensing in play, this is clearly the area needing the deepest, most concrete module content, not the thinnest.

## Allergens and dietary

The Batch House menu leans heavily into swaps: gluten free bread available on basically everything, oat milk and soy milk alongside dairy, a vegan option on about half the brunch menu via swapping the eggs for tofu scramble or the halloumi for avocado. There is no written allergen matrix. "Priya knows all of it in her head, honestly, you'd want to talk to her directly about which items have nuts in them, especially the granola and the banana bread which both have walnuts." Renata could name the obvious ones (nuts in the granola, gluten in the sourdough, dairy in basically everything unless swapped) but couldn't speak with confidence to cross contamination practices in the kitchen, or whether the fryer is shared between anything containing gluten and the (rare) gluten free hot items.

This is a real, ordinary gap rather than a manufactured one: a menu built around swappable modifiers (GF bread, dairy free milk, vegan protein swap) with no structured allergen record anywhere, relying entirely on one person's memory. Confirmed this needs Priya's direct input before any dietary content gets authored, and confirmed it will not fit neatly as a flat item-by-item list given how many items have two or three independent swap options each.

## Menu

Coffee: full espresso range plus batch filter, oat and soy milk, one seasonal syrup rotating monthly. Food: eggs (poached, scrambled, fried) with sourdough, three toastie combinations, banana bread, house granola with yoghurt, two salads, and a lunch specials board that changes "most weeks, sometimes it just doesn't change if Priya's flat out, so don't assume it's always different." Fixed core menu is around 14 items, before counting the specials board or the dietary swap combinations layered on top of most of them. Renata guessed "maybe eighteen, twenty items if you count specials" without being fully sure of the current count.

## Equipment

**Espresso machine:** three group La Marzocco, Jack's domain. Backflush done daily at close, "that one I'm confident on, Jack's strict about it." Descaling cadence was vaguer — "there's a reminder in Jack's phone, I want to say it's every few months but I couldn't tell you the exact interval off the top of my head." Water filter changeover similarly tied to a supplier visit rather than a fixed calendar date. Steam wand burn risk acknowledged as a real hazard, especially with Sofia being new — "we did show her how to steam milk properly in week one, but I don't think we've got that written down anywhere as a formal thing."

**Food prep equipment:** a mandolin slicer used for potatoes and salad veg, a stick blender, and a benchtop mixer for banana bread batter. No formal guarding or blade changing procedure exists in writing. "We just tell people to be careful with the mandolin, especially the new ones. Sofia's used it a couple of times, Liam's a bit more experienced with it." Confirmed this needs a real safety callout, not just a verbal warning passed staff to staff.

**Display cabinet:** temperature controlled, holds the banana bread, a couple of slices, and pre made sandwiches for grab and go. As noted above under food safety, the logging on this is inconsistent, stronger on weekdays than weekends. Target range wasn't quoted precisely — "it's set to whatever the fridge guy set it to when he installed it, I haven't touched the dial."

## OHS

Manual handling covers milk crate deliveries (heavy, twice a week) and moving the outdoor furniture in and out depending on weather. Chemical safety mostly relates to the glass washer and dishwasher chemicals in the kitchen, stored under the sink "same as most places, nothing special." Hazard and near miss reporting is informal — "if something happens people just tell me, there's no form for it." Steam wand burns and the mandolin blade both came up again here as the two clearest physical hazards on site, consistent with the flow's expectation that these get flagged in OHS as well as in the equipment content, not just once.

Evacuation and max occupancy: this is where the capacity contradiction surfaced, noted above under venue basics — forty two seats stated early, thirty five raised later in this context, neither number formally reconciled during the visit.

## Cash handling

Float is $200, counted at open and close by whoever's opening or closing that day. EFTPOS is the large majority of transactions, "cash is maybe five percent of what comes through now, mostly older regulars." Petty cash sits around $50 for small supply runs, sign off is just Renata approving it verbally or over text, no formal petty cash log. Safe code known to Renata, Priya, and Jack only.

## Closing procedures

Till reconciliation and EFTPOS batch close happen every night. Stock lock up covers the walk in fridge and the dry store. No CCTV on site — "never felt like we needed it, it's a pretty quiet street." Alarm is set by whoever locks up last, usually Renata or Jack. Because there's no licence, there's no last drinks or RSA compliance check step in the closing routine at all, which Renata confirmed without being prompted: "that's not really a thing for us, is it, we don't sell alcohol."

## Business continuity

Emergency contacts list exists informally in Renata's phone rather than posted anywhere on site — "I should probably print that out and stick it in the kitchen, we haven't done that yet." POS outage plan is thin: "if the EFTPOS machine dies we'd just... I don't actually know, maybe cash only until it's fixed? We haven't had it happen badly enough to need a real plan." Supplier fallback for coffee beans exists informally (a second roaster Renata knows personally as backup), but no fallback identified for the produce or bread supplier. Insurance: "we've got contents and public liability through our broker, I'd have to dig out the actual policy paperwork, I don't have the numbers in my head." Trailed off there and moved on without a firm answer on coverage limits or whether business interruption is included.

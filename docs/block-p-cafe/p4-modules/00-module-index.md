## Deliberate scope exclusion: no RSA, crowd control, liquor licensing, or happy hour content

The Batch House is confirmed unlicensed: no liquor licence, no BYO permit, and a settled, deliberate owner decision against pursuing one three years ago (per the interview, not an oversight or a "haven't gotten around to it" situation). Per the licensing gate, none of RSA, crowd controller, liquor licensing, or happy hour/promotional pricing content was authored anywhere in this module set. This is confirmed explicitly to staff in Module 01, Section 3, including the practical instruction for what to say if a customer asks about BYO or alcohol, rather than simply omitting the topic and hoping it never comes up. Writing this content in would have misrepresented a venue that genuinely has no alcohol compliance surface at all, per P1's own finding that this is a legitimate, complete answer for a coffee and food cafe, not a gap.

## Role scope reasoning

- **All roles** get Welcome, Allergens, Workplace health and safety, and Business continuity, because every role, kitchen and floor alike, needs the orientation content, fields the occasional dietary question at the till, needs the emergency and evacuation basics, and needs to know what to do if the EFTPOS system or a supplier falls over, regardless of department.
- **Food safety fundamentals and food prep equipment safety are scoped to Cook and Kitchen Hand/FOH Casual only**, matching who actually handles raw ingredients, temperature logging of the walk in fridge, the cleaning whiteboard, and the mandolin, stick blender, and mixer. Baristas don't need this content, they aren't the ones handling raw food prep.
- **Display cabinet and grab and go safety is extended to Barista/FOH as well as Cook and Kitchen Hand/FOH Casual**, since Mia is the one who actually logs the cabinet most mornings, and any FOH role might open or serve from it. This mirrors the allergen module's own logic: the people fielding a customer facing moment need the content even if they aren't the ones who prepared the food.
- **Espresso machine care and safety is scoped to Senior Barista, Barista/FOH, and Kitchen Hand/FOH Casual**, not Cook or Owner/Manager, because Jack and Mia are the two who actually run the machine day to day, and Sofia (Kitchen Hand/FOH Casual) was specifically shown steam wand technique in her first week, meaning kitchen hands do sometimes cross into barista tasks at this venue. Renata and Priya have no stated involvement with the espresso machine in the interview, so they're left off this one.
- **Cash handling and closing procedures is scoped to Owner/Manager, Senior Barista, and Barista/FOH**, matching Renata, Jack, and Mia, the three named in the interview as opening, closing, and counting the float. Priya and the kitchen hand/FOH casuals aren't described as doing this, so they're left off.

## Genuine gaps and unresolved items flagged during authoring

This venue's source interview was messier than a straightforward pass, on purpose, and this list states honestly what was found, how it was handled in the module content, and what still needs resolving before this content should be treated as final.

### A legal fact with a genuine, unresolved contradiction

1. **Seating and occupancy were given as two different figures.** "Forty two inside, eight out front" was stated early and firmly. Later, discussing a fire and evacuation scenario specifically, Renata revised this unprompted to "closer to thirty five," and nobody in the room reconciled the two numbers. This is not owner recollection the way a happy hour day or a keg count might be, maximum occupancy for a fire and evacuation situation is a real fact with an actual external answer, potentially tied to a building or fire safety occupancy figure and possibly a council registered figure for the food business, not something a working number can be inferred from memory. The two figures are also far enough apart to matter, roughly 50 seats total in the first version versus 35 in the second, more than a 30 percent difference. Module 07, Section 3 deliberately does not assert either number. It states both as given, notes neither has been formally confirmed, and directs staff to Renata for the venue's actual, confirmed figure rather than to either forty two or thirty five. **This is a pre launch verification item**: get the venue's actual, confirmed maximum occupancy figure, from whatever official source applies (a building or occupancy permit, a fire safety assessment, or a council record), before this figure is used anywhere, including in Ask Larder's knowledge base or in this module.

### Answers deferred to someone not present

2. **The exact expiry date on Priya's Food Safety Supervisor certificate is unconfirmed.** Renata knows Priya holds it and thinks it was completed roughly two years ago, but said directly she'd need to check the folder for the actual date. Module 02, Section 2 states that Priya holds the certificate and directs staff to check the exact date with Renata rather than naming a specific expiry. **Pre launch item**: confirm and record the actual renewal date before this module, or Ask Larder, states one.
3. **The kitchen's cleaning schedule breakdown lives entirely on Priya's whiteboard**, and nobody else in the room could describe it precisely beyond "it happens." Module 02, Section 5 directs staff to follow the whiteboard as it stands rather than inventing specific cleaning tasks that were never confirmed in the interview. Worth properly documenting what's actually on that schedule rather than relying on one person's undocumented knowledge of it.
4. **Cross contamination practices and shared fryer status are entirely Priya's knowledge, unconfirmed elsewhere.** Whether the fryer is shared between anything containing gluten and the venue's rare gluten free hot items was specifically flagged in the interview as something only Priya could speak to. Module 04, Section 3 states this honestly as a real, unresolved gap rather than guessing at an answer. **Needs Priya's direct input** before any more detailed cross contamination content can be written responsibly.

### Realistic regulatory looseness, documented as actual practice

5. **There is no written allergen matrix for a menu built around swappable modifiers** (GF bread, dairy free milk, a vegan protein swap), and that knowledge relies entirely on Priya's memory. This is an ordinary, realistic gap for a venue this size, not a manufactured one. Module 04 was written around confirmed facts (Section 2), an honest statement of what isn't recorded (Section 3), and an escalation process that tells staff to say "I can't confirm that right now" rather than guess (Section 4), instead of attempting a full item by item matrix. **Flagged as a real compliance risk worth closing**: a proper written allergen record, kept current as the specials board changes, would be a meaningfully stronger position than relying on one person's memory, even for a menu of this size.
6. **Weekend display cabinet temperature logging is admitted to be inconsistent** ("on weekends when it's just casuals running the floor it doesn't always happen," Renata's own words), while weekday logging is solid. Modules 02 (Section 4) and 03 (Section 3) state the required standard, logged every day, weekends included, since that genuinely is the expectation and training exists to close a gap like this, but the admitted weekend shortfall is flagged here rather than silently smoothed over. **This is a real content flag, not just a footnote**: the module alone won't fix an execution gap that's already been admitted, active reinforcement from Renata or Priya is what actually closes it.
7. **Receiving checks on deliveries are informal** ("by feel mostly, we don't log it against a number every time"). Module 02, Section 5 states the required standard, checking temperature on potentially hazardous deliveries, rather than the admittedly inconsistent current practice. **Flagged as a real compliance risk needing manager follow up**, not something this module resolves by itself.
8. **Descaling cadence and water filter changeover for the espresso machine are both vague**, tied to a phone reminder and to supplier visit timing rather than a documented schedule. Module 05, Section 2 states this honestly and directs staff to ask Jack for the current timing rather than asserting a specific interval that was never actually given. **Worth documenting properly**: a real, written maintenance schedule would be a meaningful improvement over one person's phone reminder, especially given Jack is the only one who currently knows it.
9. **Steam wand training for new starters is real but entirely undocumented.** Sofia was shown how to steam milk safely in her first week, but this isn't written down anywhere as a formal procedure. Module 05, Section 3 documents the real practice and adds a safety critical callout, since a burn hazard warrants one regardless of whether the underlying process is formally written up yet. **Worth formalising**: a short written new starter procedure for the steam wand would close this gap properly.
10. **The mandolin slicer has no formal guarding or blade changing procedure in writing**, staff are told verbally to be careful, and experience varies between Liam and Sofia. Per the instruction to give this a real safety callout rather than just a verbal warning, Module 06, Section 1 documents current practice honestly and adds a safety critical callout with a concrete instruction (use the guard or pusher hand, ask for help if inexperienced). **Flagged as worth closing properly**: a written procedure, not just staff to staff advice, would be a stronger position here.
11. **Hazard and near miss reporting is entirely informal** ("if something happens people just tell me, there's no form for it"). Module 07, Section 4 documents this as the real process rather than inventing a more rigorous system that doesn't exist. This is a practice gap more than a content gap, the instruction to tell Renata is real and workable, it just isn't backed by any written record.
12. **No formal minimum notice policy exists for shift swaps** ("no, not written down, it's just been fine so far because it's a small team"). This wasn't built into a dedicated module or section, since the actual rostering process, a shared Google Sheet published about a week ahead, with swaps handled staff to staff via group chat, is operational rather than safety or compliance content, and Module 01, Section 5 already covers who to go to for rostering questions (Renata). Flagged here for completeness rather than folded into a module, since it's a genuine informality worth Renata's own attention if the team grows.

### A structural finding on the allergen module: the same authoring choice recurred, but did not force a split here

19. **The full allergen picture again didn't fit as a written matrix, for the same underlying reason as a prior pass with a larger menu**, not because of item count this time, but because the knowledge itself is entirely unrecorded. The Batch House's menu is materially smaller, around 18 to 20 items including specials, well short of a menu that would blow out a module's section count on volume alone. What forced the same principles based treatment (confirmed facts, an honest statement of what isn't recorded, and an escalation process) wasn't length, it was that a swap heavy menu with two or three independent modifiers per item, held entirely in one absent person's head, doesn't compress into a reliable flat list regardless of how many items there are. Module 04 comfortably fits in 4 sections, well under the 6 section ceiling, so this pass did not need to split allergen content into a second module the way sheer volume might force elsewhere. **Flagged as a related but distinct structural finding**: an unrecorded, swap heavy menu is its own reason to avoid a prose matrix, separate from and additional to the sheer item count reason found previously.

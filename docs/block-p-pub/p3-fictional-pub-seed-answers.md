# Block P3 — Fictional Pub Seed Answers (Onboarding Interview Notes)

**Interview conducted across two visits, 3 and 5 September, plus a follow-up phone call to confirm a couple of things Gary wasn't sure of on the day. Notes below are compiled from what was actually said, not tidied into a clean questionnaire. Where someone deferred to a person who wasn't there, that's noted as it happened. Follow-ups needed are called out at point of use, not collected at the end.**

## Venue profile

**The Coachman's Arms Hotel**, 142 Main Hurstbridge Road, Diamond Creek VIC 3089. Trading entity is Kowalski Hospitality Group Pty Ltd, ABN 61 348 902 715. Classic suburban pub on a corner block, been there since the 80s under a few different owners, Gary's family took it over in 2016. Public bar up front with the TAB corner and a bank of keno screens, bistro out the back seating around 120, a beer garden off the side that seats another 70 to 80 plus standing room, and a small function room upstairs they hire out for birthdays and wakes. Full commercial kitchen doing counter meals lunch and dinner seven days. Beer range is solid for the area, taps into the mid teens, plus a fridge wall of bottled craft and the usual mainstream stuff, a modest but decent wine list, and a full spirits back bar. Gaming room out the back with EGMs, run somewhat separately from the main venue.

Owner/licensee: **Gary Pappas**. Venue/Duty Manager: **Dave Kowalski** (Gary's brother in law, runs the roster and most of the day to day). Head Chef: **Vinh Tran**. Sous Chef: **Meg O'Brien**. Kitchen hands: **Jayden Cook**, **Priya Nair**. Bar Supervisor: **Steph Vella**. Cellarman: **Rob "Robbo" Doyle**. Bartenders: **Ash Thompson**, **Chevy Nguyen**. Floor/FOH: **Tayla Ferguson**, **Josh Whelan**. Casual crowd controllers contracted in for weekends, not on payroll.

## Venue basics

Gary rattled off the address and ABN without needing to check anything, and was confident on the trading name versus the legal entity name once he understood the difference (took a second explaining). State is Victoria, obviously. Seating and standing numbers got vaguer fast: bistro is "120, maybe 130 if we pack the corner tables in," beer garden "70 to 80 depending on whether the heaters are out," public bar standing room he estimated at "50-ish." When asked for a total capacity figure he said "we're licensed for, I want to say 250, but honestly you'd want to check the actual licence document, I don't have it memorised." Later in the conversation, talking about a big Grand Final day, he mentioned "we've had 300 through here easy on a huge day" — which doesn't reconcile with the 250 figure, but that's what came out, and nobody in the room flagged it as a problem.

## Staffing

Roster of named roles above is accurate as of the visit, but Gary was upfront that the casual pool "fluctuates a lot," estimating "22, maybe 25 people on the books count everyone, but on any given week you'd see maybe 14 to 16 actually rostered." FOH/BOH split was easy for the obvious roles (chefs and kitchen hands = BOH, bar and floor = FOH) but he wasn't sure how to categorise the cellarman ("Robbo's kind of both, he pulls beers too when it's flat out") — left as FOH/cellar dual-tag pending Dave's input.

**Rostering.** Gary deferred this one entirely: "that's all Dave, he does it in some app, Deputy I think, I genuinely don't look at it." Didn't know how swap requests are handled or who approves them. Follow up with Dave directly.

## Licensing

General licence through VCGLR, Gary had the licence number in his phone and read it out confidently. Trading hours he gave clearly: 10am to 1am Thursday through Saturday, 10am to 11pm Sunday to Wednesday, with a late night (post midnight) authorisation attached to the Thursday to Saturday trade. Capacity, as above, is the one soft number in this section — "around 250 on paper, we run closer to 220 on a normal big night" was the second figure given, which doesn't match either of the earlier two.

**Gaming (EGM).** Yes, they hold a gaming machine entitlement, gaming room out the back with, Gary thought, "somewhere around 20 machines, could be a couple more." He was very clear this is not his department: "that whole side of things, the gaming licence, Responsible Gambling stuff, that's all handled through our accountant and a compliance mob we pay, I wouldn't try to explain it to you and I wouldn't want a new staff member's training relying on me explaining it either." Flagged as escalate to founder per the flow, not detailed further here.

Beer garden noise conditions: "there's a curfew, I want to say 10pm for the garden, we just tell people to pipe down around then, it's not like we've got a formal process, the bar staff just wander out and have a word."

## Patron capacity & crowd control

Crowd controllers are used Friday and Saturday nights, "and Thursday if there's a band on." Number of controllers varies, "two most nights, three if it's a big one." The security firm's name Gary got wrong twice: "Titan Protective, or is it Apex, one of those two, Dave deals with them directly, he'd have the contract." Licence numbers for individual controllers — not something Gary or Steph had on hand, noted as a genuine follow up. Radio/comms: controllers carry their own radios, bar staff don't currently have a shared channel with them, "which is probably something we should fix, we just yell across the bar at the moment."

Banned patron handling was one of the more honest, unpolished answers of the day: "we keep a list, it's literally a whiteboard in the office with a few names on it, if someone's a repeat problem we write them up there and door staff are supposed to check it, I won't pretend it's a proper system." No formal incident hand-off process beyond controllers telling Dave or Steph verbally at the end of a shift.

## RSA

RSA is taken seriously in the sense that all bar staff hold current RSA certificates, Steph confirmed she checks these when people start. Marshal arrangement is looser: "honestly whoever's senior on the floor that night handles it, we don't have one specific person assigned as marshal, it's whoever's running the shift." Steph added that in practice this is usually her or Dave, but there's no rostered "marshal" role as such and no formal escalation chart beyond "tell Dave."

## Food handling (full kitchen)

Vinh runs the kitchen and was in and out during the visit — some of this is secondhand via Gary and Meg rather than direct from Vinh, and it's flagged where that's the case.

**Food Safety Supervisor.** Gary said Vinh, then corrected himself: "actually hang on, I think Meg might have the more recent cert, we did a course last year and I can't remember who ended up with the current ticket, I'd need to check the folder." Not resolved during the visit.

**HACCP-style program.** Temperature logging happens, Meg said "twice a day, opening checks and again at close," though when pressed on whether that's written down consistently she said "we're pretty good on dinner service, breakfast prep sometimes gets missed if it's chaotic." Cleaning schedule exists as a whiteboard roster in the kitchen that Vinh maintains — nobody else in the room could describe exactly what's on it. Receiving checks were the least reassuring answer of the visit: "the delivery guy hands it over, someone signs for it, I honestly couldn't tell you if we're checking temps on every delivery, probably not every single one if I'm being honest" — Meg said this, not defensively, just matter-of-fact.

**Allergen tagging.** Menu runs to roughly 34 items once you count mains, sides, sauces, and the rotating specials board, which Gary said changes "every couple of weeks, sometimes more if Vinh gets bored." Allergen information exists informally — Vinh apparently keeps a mental map of what has nuts/gluten/dairy — but there's no written matrix anyone could produce on the spot. Gary: "staff mostly just know to ask the kitchen if someone asks about allergies, we haven't written it all out formally, that's probably something we should do properly rather than winging it."

## Menus

Food menu: parma variations, schnitzels, a burger range, a couple of pasta dishes, salads, kids menu, the rotating specials board mentioned above. Drinks: full spirits range, house wine list of about a dozen by the glass, and a cocktail list Steph said is "eight, maybe nine now that we added the espresso martini properly, it used to just be the classics." Tap beer range covered more fully under Equipment/Cellar below since it overlaps heavily with that conversation.

## Happy hour / promotional pricing

Yes, they run happy hour. Early in the visit Gary described it as "Tuesday to Thursday, 5 to 7, house tap beer and house wine, two dollars off." Later, when the conversation moved to weekend trade patterns, he said, without seeming to notice any conflict with what he'd said earlier, "well the real happy hour crowd is just Thursday and Friday arvo, that's when the tradies knock off and come in, Tuesday's usually dead so I don't know why we even bother." Both versions are recorded as given; which one reflects the actual current policy versus what's on paper somewhere is a genuine open question, not something resolved on the day.

No specific promotional constraints were mentioned beyond "we don't do anything silly like free drinks or 2-for-1 shots, just a couple of bucks off tap beer and house wine," which Gary offered unprompted when asked if he was aware of any rules around discounted drink pricing. Whether that's compliant with any Victorian promotional-pricing rule wasn't something he'd thought about specifically — worth checking once the Victoria pub research doc lands.

## Equipment & stations (cellar/keg-at-volume)

Cellar is a dedicated room off the back of the public bar. Tap count: Gary said "14, I think, might be 13, Robbo would know exactly," and Robbo wasn't on site during either visit to confirm — genuine follow up needed here, not fully across this. Mix of rotating craft lines and fixed mainstream lines, Steph estimated "maybe four or five of the fourteen rotate, the rest are pretty much always the same beers."

CO2 storage: cylinders are kept in the cellar itself, "bunch of them, I couldn't tell you the exact number off the top of my head," according to Dave when he popped in briefly. Gas safety training for staff beyond Robbo wasn't clearly described — Dave said "Robbo's across all that, cylinder changeovers, the safety side of it, I wouldn't want to give you the wrong info on gas handling, better to get it straight from him."

Line cleaning cadence: "every two weeks I want to say, could be monthly, honestly Robbo's got a schedule for it, I just know it happens," from Dave again. Keg changeovers are Robbo's job day to day, with Ash or Chevy covering if he's off.

## OHS

Manual handling: no formal training program identified, Gary said "we tell people to lift with their knees, it's pretty ad hoc, we probably should have something more structured for the kegs especially." Chemical/cleaning safety: cleaning chemicals are stored in a locked cupboard behind the kitchen with the original labelled containers, Meg confirmed this much confidently. Glass collection is a FOH task shared across whoever's on the floor, no dedicated glassy role. Hazard/near miss reporting: "there's a folder in the office for incident reports, it barely gets used if I'm honest, most stuff just gets sorted verbally on the night and forgotten about," Gary said, which is more candid than confidence-inspiring but is what actually happens.

## Cash handling

Float is $500 across the two tills, confirmed clearly by both Gary and Steph. Safe drops happen roughly hourly on a busy night, done by whoever's the duty manager on shift. EFTPOS reconciliation is done nightly against the till by the closing manager. Safe code itself is restricted to Gary, Dave, and Steph — nobody else has it, which Gary was firm about.

**Petty cash.** Anything under $50 doesn't need sign off, Dave or Gary sign off anything above that, kept in a small lock box in the office. This one came through clean and consistent.

## Closing procedures

Till reconciliation and stock lock-up done by the closing duty manager (usually Dave, sometimes Steph). Alarm code is restricted the same way as the safe code. Last drinks timing had a small wrinkle: Steph said "we call last drinks about 12:30 for a 1am close," while Tayla, who was also in the room, said she thought it was "12:45, maybe," and neither seemed particularly bothered by the discrepancy — likely just reflects that it varies a bit by how busy the night is rather than a fixed rule. CCTV review only happens reactively: "if something's happened we'll go back and look, we're not sitting there watching it every night," Gary said.

## Security

Bag checks happen on busier nights at the door when crowd controllers are on, not a standing policy every night. ID scanning at the door is used Friday and Saturday nights only. Duress alarm exists under the main bar till, Steph confirmed its location clearly and said staff are shown it in their first week. Banned patron handling is the same whiteboard-list system described under Patron Capacity above — folded into this section rather than repeated as a separate formal process, since that's genuinely all there is.

## Business continuity

Emergency contacts exist for an electrician and the POS provider (a Square-based system, Gary thinks, "or something similar, Dave set that up"), but Gary didn't have the insurance broker's details on him: "that's on an email somewhere, Dave or my accountant would have it, I don't carry that number around." Supplier fallback for kegs running dry was the most concrete answer in this whole section: "if we're about to run out of something on a big night we've literally just rung the pub up the road and borrowed a keg off them before, we'd pay it back the next delivery," which Gary said with a laugh, clearly treating it as a normal thing rather than an actual documented contingency plan.

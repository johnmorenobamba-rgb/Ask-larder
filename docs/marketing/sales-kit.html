01

Qualifying questions

Ask these before you pitch anything. Almost any independent venue with 10 to 30 staff is a fit, the goal is finding out which pain to lead with.

  1. How many staff do you currently have, and how often do you hire?Tells you the pricing tier, and how often the onboarding pain actually recurs.
  2. Walk me through what happens on a new hire's first shift right now.Listen for a folder, a PDF nobody reads, or a senior staff member pulled off the floor.
  3. How do you currently track RSA, food handling, first aid, and Working with Children checks?A spreadsheet, a wall calendar, someone's memory, or an honest "we don't, really."
  4. When a staff member has a question mid shift and a manager isn't around, what do they do?Listen for how often the same senior person gets pulled away from real work.
  5. What's the most frustrating part of running training and onboarding here today?Open ended. Let them talk. Don't lead with pricing or mechanism before you've heard this.
02

The 30 second pitch

Say this out loud. Adjust the pain to whatever came out of qualifying.

Say it like this

"Larder turns your own SOPs, your own photos, and how your own staff actually work into training built specifically for your venue, not generic content. New hires get an always on chatbot that answers from your procedures only, so whoever currently gets interrupted isn't stuck re​explaining the same thing every week. You get a dashboard tracking who's finished training and when every certificate expires, with reminders before they lapse. Nothing goes live until you've reviewed it yourself. And it's month to month, no lock in, so there's no risk in trying it."

04

Objection handling

Answer honestly, grounded in the real product and pricing. Don't deflect, don't oversell.

ObjectionHonest answer
"Too expensive for a small venue."Setup scales with size: $799 for one to five staff, $1,299 for six to fifteen, $1,999 for sixteen to twenty five, custom for anything larger. The monthly retainer is a flat $100 and includes tracking, reminders, and five edits a month. A discretionary founder's rate of $750 exists for a first venue.
"We already train staff fine informally."That might be true while the same senior people are around. The question is what happens the day one of them is off, sick, or leaves.
"AI will just make things up."It only answers from that venue's own approved content, filtered at the database level. It can't pull from another venue or invent an answer, and always defers keys, safes, and logins to a supervisor.
"What happens to our data if we cancel?"Month to month, 30 days' written notice, full export of content and records. Nothing gets held hostage, by design.
"This feels like a big process change."Built from SOPs, photos, and short interviews, not a long implementation. Nothing goes live until the owner approves every module.
"How do I know this works before committing?"A real, live demo venue on the actual product. It can be shown live, or a link and a login sent together to try hands on afterward. The link alone won't get anyone in.
05

Presenting pricing

A straightforward, honest number, not a reveal at the end of a script. Bring it up once the mechanism lands. State it, then stop talking.

First conversation

  • The setup fee tier matching their staff count, stated plainly
  • The $100/month retainer, and exactly what it includes
  • Month to month, 30 days' notice, no lock in
  • The founder's rate, if they're a strong first client

Can wait for follow up

  • The $15 per additional edit rate
  • The complimentary quiz add on
  • SOP generation quoting, after a walkthrough
  • The Large tier custom quote

Sample call

One way it goes, not the only way.

John

How many staff have you got, and how often are you hiring?

Owner

About eighteen, and honestly we're always onboarding someone.

John

When someone new starts, what actually happens on their first shift?

Owner

Whoever's on shows them around. It's fine, but not exactly consistent.

John

That's really common, it's actually what got me building this. I'm a working chef, I built Larder because I needed exactly this. Thirty second version: it turns your own procedures into training built for your venue, with a chatbot that only answers from your own approved material, and nothing goes live until you've signed off on it.

Owner

Won't the AI just make things up if someone asks it something it doesn't know?

John

Good question, first thing I'd ask too. It's enforced at the database level, not a prompt telling it to behave. I can show you a live demo right now if you want to try asking it something.

Owner

What's this cost?

John

For eighteen staff you'd be mid tier, $1,999 setup and $100 a month. Since you'd be one of my first venues, I'd offer the founder's rate instead, $750, to get this proven out properly.

Owner

Can I see it working first?

John

Absolutely, best way to judge it. I'll send the demo link, have a play before we talk again.

07

FAQ

Is this a full replacement for in person training?

No. It supports in person training and hands off anything requiring physical access to a real supervisor.

Does the chatbot know things about other venues?

No. Retrieval is filtered to that specific venue before anything reaches the chatbot.

Who writes the training content?

Built from the venue's own SOPs, photos, and interviews. The owner approves every module before it goes live.

Does this work for a venue with nothing written down?

Yes, that's a common starting point. The build process creates that documentation for the first time.

Is RSA or WWCC content the same everywhere in Australia?

No. Requirements are state and territory specific, confirmed against that venue's actual state.

What's the e‑signature actually for?

A staff member typing their name to confirm completion, with timestamp and device recorded. A comprehension record, not a legal contract signature.

How long does setup actually take?

Not yet standardised, there's no completed first client to benchmark against. Be honest about this if asked.

08

Keeping this current

A living document, updated on triggers, not a fixed schedule.

Once there's a real first client

  • Add one genuine, specific case study, only once a venue has gone live and used the product for a few weeks. Real words only, with permission.
  • Replace the honest "no case study yet" framing with a real before and after. Only real numbers, never projected ones.
  • Keep the founder's rate language until there's a defensible reason to phase it out.

For a new tier, venue type, or region

  • Re run the qualifying questions with someone who actually represents that segment.
  • Re check every compliance claim against that region's actual regulator.
  • Re verify every price against the current Notion Decision Log before reusing this document.
  • Keep the same structure. It's what makes this usable in a real conversation.

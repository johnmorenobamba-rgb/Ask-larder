## Section 1: Supplier and trade backups

We keep a backup keg supplier and a backup spirits supplier on file, both live accounts rather than just a name on a card, in case our primary distributor's delivery is delayed. If you notice a delivery hasn't turned up when expected, flag it to the Duty Manager rather than assuming it'll sort itself out.

For anything physical that breaks, Brunswick Electrical Services covers electrical issues, Nicholson St Plumbing covers plumbing, and Merri Locksmiths runs a 24 hour line for lockouts or lock issues. These contacts are kept in the office folder along with our insurance broker's details.

## Section 2: Who to go to, insurance, and the regulator

Day to day, hazards, near misses, and anything operational go to whoever's Duty Manager on shift. Anything involving the licence itself, like a regulator query, goes through Marcus, who deals directly with Liquor Control Victoria, the state body that regulates our licence.

If something happens that could turn into an insurance claim, a break in, a flood, storm damage, or a significant injury, Marcus is the one who calls the broker, not the Duty Manager on shift. Flag it to Marcus as soon as it's safe to do so, rather than calling the broker yourself even if you know where the policy documents are kept.

Insurance details are kept with the physical policy documents in the office, referenced by policy number rather than repeated in training, so if you ever need them, that's where to point someone, but the actual call to the broker goes through Marcus.

## Section 3: Reopening after an unplanned closure

If The Quiet Fox has to close unexpectedly, a power outage that ran long, a flood, storm damage, or any incident serious enough to shut the floor, Marcus decides when it's safe to reopen. No one else makes that call, even if the immediate problem looks fixed.

Before reopening, the Duty Manager checks three things first: that the under bar fridge has held at or below 5°C for long enough that its contents are safe to serve again, that the premises itself is physically safe to have guests back in, power restored, water working, nothing damaged that could hurt someone, and that anything reported to insurance or the regulator has actually been dealt with, not just reported.

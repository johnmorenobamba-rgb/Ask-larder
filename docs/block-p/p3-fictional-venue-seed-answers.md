## 1. Venue basics

Marcus runs The Quiet Fox as a single venue, no group structure. Legal name on the lease and licence is The Quiet Fox Bar Pty Ltd, trading simply as The Quiet Fox. Address is 212 Nicholson Street, Brunswick East. ABN 54 187 622 903. Confirmed Victoria as the operating state before continuing into the licensing branch.

Layout: the main room runs booth seating down one wall (24 seats across six booths) and high tables down the other (16 seats), with standing room for about 30 around the main bar and the front window. The back nook (12 seats) is used for small function bookings and closed off with a curtain rail when not booked. The footpath strip out front holds 8 of the 30 standing patrons on a fine night, inside the licensed conditions below.

## 2. Staffing

Real role names used day to day: Duty Manager, Bar Supervisor, Bar Attendant, Glassie. Marcus doesn't use "Bartender" as a title internally, staff and rosters all say Bar Attendant, so that's the name going into training.

Every role above is FOH. There's no kitchen and no BOH department tag needed anywhere in the roster.

No staff under 18 are rostered — Marcus's hiring floor is 18 regardless of role, so there's no split between roles that can and can't serve. That's stated as a blanket policy rather than a role-by-role carve-out.

Full roster for invites: Priya Nandakumar (Duty Manager), Jordan Ashcroft (Bar Supervisor), Liv Tran, Sam Okafor, Dee Whitfield (Bar Attendants), Noah Fitzgerald (Glassie). Marcus will hold the owner login himself and isn't going through staff onboarding.

## 3. Liquor licensing

Licence type: **on-premises licence**, liquor tied to meals/function use. Marcus confirmed this directly, not "General" and not unsure — the on-premises category is why the snacks menu exists at all, it's what lets the bar trade as a bar rather than needing a full restaurant fit-out.

Licensed patron capacity: **70** (40 seated, 30 standing, as above).

Approved trading hours:
- Tuesday to Wednesday: 4pm to 11pm
- Thursday: 4pm to midnight
- Friday to Saturday: 3pm to 1am
- Sunday: 2pm to 10pm
- Monday: closed

No late-night endorsement, which is consistent with the 1am cap on the latest trading nights — nothing on this licence goes past 1am and nothing should ever be trained or scheduled past it.

Area/outdoor conditions: the footpath trading strip (8 seats) is permitted only until 10pm, and there's a no-amplified-music-outdoors condition after 8pm to manage noise complaints from the apartments above the shops next door. Both conditions were flagged specifically because they're easy for a new Duty Manager to forget on a warm Thursday when the crowd wants to spill outside.

## 4. Patron capacity & crowd control

At 70 capacity and a 1am latest close, The Quiet Fox sits below the threshold where crowd controllers become mandatory, and Marcus has made a deliberate call not to run them electively either — he says the room's small enough that Priya or Jordan can read the floor themselves, and the venue's incident history (a handful of overservice refusals a year, nothing physical) doesn't justify the cost. So this goes down the "No" branch: basic conflict de-escalation content only, no dedicated security module, no crowd-controller detail to capture.

## 5. RSA

RSA certification is required for every role that pours or carries drinks to a table, which at this venue is all of them, including Noah as Glassie — he regularly runs drinks from the bar to the booths when it's busy, so Marcus wants him RSA-certified even though he's not usually the one pouring.

Individual RSA certificates will be captured per staff member (photo of the card, issue date, expiry date) as each person is invited.

The venue does designate an RSA marshal: **Priya Nandakumar**, in her capacity as Duty Manager. She's rostered as marshal on every Friday and Saturday night, and on any other night the floor count goes over 50. Her escalation authority: she can override any Bar Attendant's decision to keep serving a patron and direct an immediate refusal, and she's the one who has the final word if a Bar Attendant and a patron disagree about whether someone's had enough.

Refusal script the venue actually uses, word for word as staff are trained to say it: *"I can't serve you another one, I want to make sure you get home okay tonight. Can I grab you a water, or call you a taxi?"* Staff are told to stay on that script rather than improvise, and to call Priya or Jordan over immediately if a patron pushes back.

ID-checking standard: staff check ID for anyone who looks under 25, no exceptions, no "you look old enough" judgement calls left to individual staff.

Intoxication signs staff are trained to watch for: slurred speech, unsteady balance, spilling drinks, becoming loud or argumentative, or losing track of a conversation mid-sentence. Standard stuff, covered in the general RSA module content rather than anything venue-specific.

## 6. Food handling & allergens

Food service level: **bar snacks only, packaged/low-risk**. No cooking equipment on site, nothing hot leaves the bar. The menu is cold boards and packaged items only: marinated olives, marinated feta, kettle chips, pretzels, a cheese board (pre-cut soft and hard cheese, assembled to order), and a charcuterie board (pre-sliced cured meats, also assembled to order). Because this sits at the low-risk end, Marcus does not have a nominated Food Safety Supervisor and confirmed he understands that's not required at this risk class, unlike if he ever added a cooked menu.

Light food handling content covers: cold items held in the under-bar fridge at or below 5°C, boards assembled to order rather than pre-made and held out, hands washed and gloves changed between board types (particularly nuts and dairy), and stock rotated so nothing sits in the display fridge past its use-by.

Allergen matrix, tagged per item:
- Marinated olives: none flagged
- Marinated feta: dairy
- Kettle chips: none flagged
- Pretzels: gluten
- Cheese board: dairy, and a note that the accompanying lavash is gluten
- Charcuterie board: may contain traces of nuts (shared prep space with the cheese board), gluten in the accompanying bread

## 7. Menus

Drinks list: 12 cocktails on the core list, rotated seasonally, an 8-wine by-the-glass list, four tap beer lines plus a bottled selection, and a small non-alcoholic list of three mocktails for designated drivers and non-drinkers.

Standard pours: spirits at a 30ml nip, wine at 150ml standard pour or 120ml small pour, beer in schooners and pints off the tap. Staff are trained on these as the baseline for both consistency and RSA (knowing what a standard drink actually looks like when judging how much someone's had).

Food menu: the same six snack items listed under food handling above, each carrying its allergen tag. No separate food menu beyond the boards, since there's no kitchen.

Cleaning/operating procedure exists per piece of equipment (glasswasher rinse-aid and descale schedule, beer line cleaning on a fortnightly rotation, cool room defrost check) and will sit against each station once QR codes are assigned.

## 9. OHS

Manual handling: keg changes are the main risk — full 50L kegs run close to 58kg, so the venue trains a two-person lift for anything coming up from the cool room, never a solo lift on a full keg. Glass collection follows a scoop-and-carry method with rubber-lined tubs rather than bare hands through broken glass.

Chemical/cleaning safety covers the glasswasher detergent and sanitiser used nightly, stored in their original containers under the back bar, never decanted into unlabelled bottles.

Hazard/near-miss escalation: reported to whoever's the Duty Manager on shift at the time, logged in the near-miss folder, and reviewed weekly by Marcus. There's no after-hours emergency escalation beyond that, since nothing at this venue runs unsupervised.

---

## 10. Cash handling

Float: $400 total opening float, split as $250 on the main bar till and $150 on the second till, which only gets opened on Friday and Saturday nights when the second Square terminal comes online.

Till reconciliation: each till is counted down at the end of the night against its Square Z-read. Anything over a $10 discrepancy either way gets flagged to the Duty Manager and noted in the reconciliation log before anyone leaves.

Safe drop procedure: on Friday and Saturday after 8pm, whenever a till holds more than $500 in cash, the Duty Manager does a drop — cash counted, bagged, and logged against the till reading with the Duty Manager's initials, then into the safe.

EFTPOS reconciliation: the nightly Square settlement report is cross-checked against the physical till count as part of close, not left until the morning.

Who has the safe code and how it's shared: only Marcus and the two people who hold the Duty Manager role (currently Priya, plus a trained backup for when Priya's off) know the combination. It's never written down anywhere, including in any handover notes. When someone new steps into the Duty Manager role, Marcus gives them the code in person, and the combination is changed every quarter regardless of whether anyone's left. Module content and Ask Larder are both to point anyone asking for the actual code back to their supervisor rather than ever stating it.

## 11. Closing procedures

Closing runs in this order, every night, no matter how quiet:

1. Last drinks called 30 minutes before the licensed close time for that night; no new drinks served after the close time itself.
2. Till reconciliation and Square Z-read on both tills in use that night.
3. Stock lock-up — spirits cage locked, cool room locked, kegs disconnected only if a keg's due to be swapped before the next shift.
4. Quick CCTV review of the night, checking specifically for any incident that was flagged verbally during service.
5. Alarm set.
6. Final walk-through, lights off, front and back doors locked.

Who's authorised to close: Duty Manager or Bar Supervisor (2IC) only. A Bar Attendant is never left to close alone, even on a quiet Tuesday.

---

## 12. Security

Since crowd controllers aren't used, security content folds into closing procedures as a lighter section rather than standing alone.

CCTV: six cameras covering the entry, main bar, back bar, function nook, back door, and the footpath trading strip. Footage is retained 30 days and the Duty Manager gives it a quick review each closing night for anything flagged during service, with a fuller review if an actual incident occurred.

Duress alarm: a silent duress button fitted under the main till, wired to a monitored security service (Vanguard Monitoring), who dispatch if it's triggered.

Banned-patron handling: kept informally — a shared folder behind the bar with a photo and a short note on anyone who's been asked not to return, checked by whoever's on the door or behind the bar if a face looks familiar. Not a digital list, just the folder.

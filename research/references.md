## Synthesis: pacing and structure rules

Rules marked (V) are backed by something verified above; (E) are my estimate from the pattern.

1. Run 30 to 40 seconds. The strongest short refs sit at 0:30 to 0:39 (V: Raycast, Oura, Pixel, Dyson). Anything wanting more depth becomes a second asset (V: Oura's 46s companion).
2. One idea per scene, 3 to 4 scenes per 10s at most. Beats of roughly 2.5 to 4 seconds. Never a montage of micro cuts. (E)
3. Open on the real product within the first 3 seconds, not a logo or a problem skit. Fragments or one live task. (V: Raycast fragments; E: timing)
4. Make the feature demonstrate itself. The chatbot answering and refusing is the demo; no narrator describing it. (V: Pixel; Ngram on Superhuman)
5. Hold the camera on one continuous action per beat (type, tap, Stamp lands) rather than cutting around it. Motion inside the frame, not between frames. (E)
6. One colour, one meaning, readable muted. Design for sound off. (V: Dyson). Captions or on screen text as the primary track; sound design secondary and minimal.
7. Interface fills the frame and stays legible at phone size. No device mockup chrome unless it earns space. (V: Linear Initiatives)
8. End on one object and one line: the Stamp plus the wordmark, then a plain action line. Hold 2 to 3 seconds in stillness. No offer, no price, no urgency. (E)

Suggested Larder beat sheet (ESTIMATE, for the 35s version): 0 to 4s a SOP page fragment, plain statement of the problem in one line; 4 to 12s that page becoming a module, owner approval step visible; 12 to 20s new hire answers a check question, Stamp; 20 to 28s Ask Larder: a question answered, then a keys question that returns the supervisor line; 28 to 33s owner dashboard, one cert expiring in Preserve Red; 33 to 38s wordmark + one line.

## What the weak "TV shopping ad" register does, which these avoid

- Shouting a number or discount ("only $X", "limited time", countdown timers).
- Voiceover that narrates what is already visible on screen.
- Upbeat stock music with a drop on every cut and whooshes/zooms on every transition.
- Stock footage of smiling people high fiving, or a cartoon pain montage ("Tired of messy onboarding?").
- Before/after problem skit with a frustrated actor, then a rescue.
- Feature lists with icons and bullet pop ins, "and that's not all".
- Confetti, checkmarks, starbursts, sparkle effects at completion (Larder already bans these; the Stamp replaces them).
- Superlatives and claims with no proof ("revolutionary", "#1", "game changer").
- Device mockups spinning in 3D, gratuitous camera shake or glitch transitions.
- Testimonial walls and logo strips before the product has been shown.
- A call to action stacked three times (URL, phone, QR, "act now").
- Dashes, ampersands and filler in on screen copy (Larder rule: no dashes in copy, use "9am to 5pm").
